I woke up at 5am last week to go shoot content in the sunrise. It was dark when I picked up my friend Dave, who was going to be the talented man behind the lens.

We decided to shoot on a cliffside overlooking Santa Barbara.

The scene was absolutely breathtaking. Cotton candy skies, small blue dotted clouds on the horizon, the ocean spray glowing warm peach tones as it was backlit by the sun.

I had never been to this spot before at sunrise. And lo and behold, it was shooting content that got me there finally.

I find it funny that I’ve lived here for two years, and it took needing to make an Instagram video to get me out there at five in the morning.

Such is the state of the music industry!

It made me think of how many amazing experiences I’ve had as side-effects of releasing music.

Making lifelong friends, touring around the country in a van, playing shows to thousands of people, building something I’m proud of over the course of years...

I’m even remembering now that Sophie and I reconnected when I posted on Instagram about releasing music again.

Truly so many blessings have come from putting my work out there, even when the vast majority of it didn’t have the “success” I was hoping for in my mind.

As they say in the music industry:

<MarginNote src={noteTrueLove} side="center" alt="Handwritten in red: “True love is better than a billion streams.”" />

(Just kidding, they definitely don’t say that).

It makes me think about why I make music in the first place.

When I started, I was mesmerized by all the shiny rewards you could get if you “made it” and “became famous”.

Now, I’m 31 (almost 32), which in the music industry is like 97. I have different motivations now.

In a lot of ways though, I’m more motivated to release than ever. I think I’ve started to notice that making music and releasing it makes me a better person (hopefully others think so too).

Writing is hard. Releasing music is hard. Making content is hard.

But every time I do finish a song, I think it changes me slightly. It requires me to be a little more honest. Dig a little deeper. Search a little bit harder for the truth.

Now I think that’s way more exciting.

<MarginNote src={horse} kind="doodle" side="left" size="9rem" alt="A small red drawing of a galloping horse." />

When I first started releasing music, it was a little bit of an act. I was writing edgy blues rock, which I love, but the lyrics, and the energy behind it wasn’t truly who I was.

  src={show}

It wasn’t completely off the mark, but I’ve had to accept that who I really am isn’t that guy.

The real me is a little cornier, likes to joke around a lot, wants to live a low-key life with his family, and enjoys being outdoors.

It’s been said a million times in the music industry: “Be authentic.”

For me it was a lot harder than that sounds.

How can you be authentic if you don’t really know who you are? Or what you’re made of? At least that was my problem.

I found out who I was by creating. By doing. By releasing.

If I was being myself in the process, it became enjoyable and sustainable.

Being myself also led me to people who share my vision. Making music with a team is way more rewarding than doing it alone, and I never could have gotten here by myself. Shoutout to Dave, Josh, Tom, Brett, Brielle and so many more!

The funny thing is, now that I’m less interested in becoming the artist I thought I needed to be, more people are listening than ever.

Together, we passed 800,000 monthly listeners on Spotify this month. That would’ve blown my mind when I was in the band. A lot of you are part of that number too, so thank you for listening!

I don’t know how much of that has to do with any of this. But I do know I’m enjoying making music more than I ever have.

I can’t help but think it’s because the music seems more true now.

In the spirit of that, next week I’m releasing a new song that feels more like me than ever.

It’s a little on the nose, a little earnest, but hey, I think maybe that’s who I am.
